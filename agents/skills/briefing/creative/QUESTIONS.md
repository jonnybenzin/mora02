# Question catalogue

Generic, and meant as a starting point. What a brief really needs shows itself
in real briefs — this list is here to be rewritten, not to be obeyed. The order
is deliberate: first what for, then for whom, then what.

The agent asks them **one at a time** and **in its own words**. Reading them
out conducts an interview instead of a conversation.

---

## 1. Occasion

What is this about, and what set it off? A client, a date, a campaign, an idea
of your own?

*Push back if only an object is named ("a video") and no occasion.*

## 2. Goal

What should be different afterwards? Who should do, think or know what?

*This is the question most briefs stay thin on. "Attention" is not a goal.
"Sign-ups by the 30th" is one.*

## 3. Audience

Who sees this, and in what situation? On a phone in passing, or at a desk with
time to spare?

## 4. Message

If only one sentence sticks — which one?

## 5. Format and channel

What exactly is being made, where does it run, what dimensions and what length?

*Push back if the channel is missing: the same picture does not work the same
way everywhere.*

## 6. Tone

How should it feel? And is there something existing to take a lead from — or to
set itself apart from?

## 7. Mandatory

What has to appear? Logo, claim, legal wording, a particular product, a
particular person.

## 8. Excluded

What must not appear under any circumstances? Motifs, words, colours,
comparisons, competitors.

*Almost never volunteered, and the most expensive thing to find out late.*

## 9. Material

What is already there — images, texts, logos, earlier versions? And where?

## 10. Deadline and constraints

By when, and is there a frame (budget, effort, number of variants)?

## 11. Sign-off

Who approves, and who else has a say?

*The question forgotten most often, and the most expensive one to be missing.*
