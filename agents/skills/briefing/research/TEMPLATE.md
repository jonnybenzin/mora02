# Template — research brief

The target structure. It is at the same time the order a research agent will
later work from — whatever stays empty here has to be guessed there.

**The fence lines below (```) bound the example only. They do not belong in the
result, and the word after them belongs there even less.**

---

```markdown
# Research brief: <short title>

**Date:** <date>

## The question
<One sentence. An answer has to be able to fit it.>

## What for
<Which decision hangs on the answer. This steers the depth.>

## Known already, and the constraints
<What is already known or checked. Plus the hard constraints the result has to
fit into: hardware (VRAM, storage), environment, budget. They sift more
sharply than any judgement of quality.>

## Angles to search from
- <term, manufacturer, opposing position, English equivalent …>

## Sources
**Counts:** <test reports, trade articles, manufacturer figures …>
**Does not count:** <advertising, forums, outdated states …>

## Time window
<How current a figure has to be. "doesn't matter" is a valid answer.>

## Region and language
<Market/country, languages to search in.>

## Scope
<Overview or thorough. If it can be put in numbers: how many sources at least.>

## Shape of the result
<Recommendation / table with the columns … / list with links / summary.>

## Not part of the question
- <expressly excluded>

## Stopping rule
<How to tell the question has been answered. If unsettled:
"open — whoever researches decides for themselves when to stop".>

## Assumed
- <field> — <what it follows from. One sentence is enough.>

## Open
- <field> — <what is missing, who would have to be asked>
```

---

**Three states, not two.** A field is answered, **assumed**, or open. Anything
assumed carries `(assumed)` after the value and also appears in the section
below — then whoever ordered the work corrects it at a glance, instead of
discovering it later in the result. Filling a field with `open` although the
answer follows from the question is not caution: it pushes the same inference
onto whoever does the research, unwritten and unchecked.

**Two fields carry the weight.** *Sources* settles every later argument about
the result, and *stopping rule* is the difference between a job and a wish.
Both may stay `open` — but visibly open, not filled with something plausible.

The `Open` section stays in place even when it is empty; then with the word
"nothing". A template whose gap list disappears as soon as there are no gaps
looks the same for an incomplete brief as for a complete one.
